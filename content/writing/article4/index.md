The fog rolled in thick that evening, as it always did in early autumn. Maya stood at the edge of the cliff, her weathered notebook clutched against her chest, watching the beam from the old lighthouse cut through the grey soup below.

![alt text](image.png)

She had found the bottle three days ago, wedged between the rocks at low tide. Inside was a letter, written in careful script on paper that had somehow stayed dry for what must have been decades.

*To whoever finds this,*

*I have kept this light burning for forty years. Every night, without fail. I have seen ships pass in the dark, their crews never knowing how close they came to the rocks. I have watched storms rage and seas calm. I have been alone, but never lonely.*

*If you are reading this, I am gone. But the light must continue. Not for the ships—they have radar now, GPS, technologies I cannot fathom. The light must continue for the same reason we tell stories, write letters, leave messages in bottles.*

*To prove we were here. To reach across time. To say: I existed, I mattered, I kept the darkness at bay.*

*Keep your own light burning, friend.*

Maya closed her notebook. Tomorrow, she would begin writing her response. Not to the lighthouse keeper—he was long gone. But to whoever would find her words, decades hence, when the lighthouse was just a memory and she was merely a name in an old maritime record.

She turned toward home as the beam swept past her once more, steady and sure, reaching into the infinite dark.
